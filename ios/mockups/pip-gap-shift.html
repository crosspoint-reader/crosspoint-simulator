The gap the chassis asks for

The panel-to-top-row gap is not a taste value — it is a measurement. On the real X3 the front buttons' top edge sits 11.6 mm below the panel window, on a 78.2 mm panel: 14.8% of panel height. That ratio is what kPanelGapRatio stores, so the proportion survives any device scale, and at the Air's presented panel it comes out as the 78.3 pt in use today. Measured off Xteink's straight-on product renders; the full table is in the firmware repo's docs/hardware-dimensions.md.

So every point of shift is a millimetre of chassis fidelity spent. Read the right-hand column as "what this gap would be on real hardware":

Under about 4 mm the pad stops reading as a separate control surface below the page and starts reading as a border around it — that is the point where the resemblance is not being reduced, it is being abandoned. 48 pt of clearance sits at 5.5 mm, which is the last stop on the near side of that line.

What it survives

The window is resizable by pinch, so the number that matters is not today's clearance but how wide the window can get before it eats the top row. At the shipped position that limit is 50% of screen width — and the window in your screenshot is already at 47.1%.

Computed from layoutPad() in ios/CrossPointIOSShim.cpp, iPhone Air at 420×912 pt, measured top inset 74 pt. Window geometry measured off the reference screenshot: 197.8 × 111.3 pt, 10.3 pt in from the right, 45.0 pt up from the bottom. Shift is one constant — kPanelGapRatio.
